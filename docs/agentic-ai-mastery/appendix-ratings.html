Ratings matrix

Every rated concept in one sortable table.

How to read the ratings

Every concept in this manual carries two independent scores. Keeping them separate is the whole point: a concept can be beautifully implemented and barely matter, or crudely implemented and absolutely load-bearing. Reading them together is how you decide where to spend effort.

Impl — implementation depth

9–10 wired into the live path, governed, tested, exposed. 7–8 real and on the live path, one dimension short. 5–6 works but opt-in or narrowly wired. 3–4 adapter or scaffold. 1–2 named but effectively absent.

Criticality — does production need it?

9–10 ship without it and you get an incident, a breach, or an unexplained bill. 7–8 needed once you have real tenants. 5–6 materially improves quality or cost. 3–4 situational. 1–2 research interest.

On honesty

These are one engineer's readings of the code as it stands, not a vendor scorecard or a benchmark. The low scores are the most useful entries in the book — they are the work that is left. Read the frontier chapter (33) for the pattern the low scores form.

How to use the matrix below. Sort mentally by the two columns: high-impl/high-crit is the platform's solid core (trust it); low-impl/high-crit is the priority backlog (build it next); high-impl/low-crit is where effort may have outrun need (research-grade luxuries); low-impl/low-crit is the honest frontier (interesting, not urgent). Filter by name, chapter, kind, or status to find any concept fast.
