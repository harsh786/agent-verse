Orientation

An agent platform accumulates data everywhere — documents, chunks, embeddings, memory, traces, screenshots, transcripts, audit — and most of it describes real people. The data lifecycle is the discipline of knowing what you hold, why, where, for how long, and being able to prove you deleted it. It is where the platform meets the law, and where "we'll add compliance later" becomes a re-architecture.

The organising idea is classification drives everything downstream. Once a piece of data is classified — public, internal, confidential, restricted — its handling, retention, residency, and deletion follow automatically. Classify at ingestion and the rest of the lifecycle is enforcement; skip it and every later obligation becomes a manual hunt.

The lifecycle

01Data classification#

ShippedFoundationImpl8/10Criticality10/10

Every datum is tagged — public, internal, confidential, restricted — at creation, and that tag drives handling, retention, residency, and deletion for the rest of its life.

Mechanism

A classifier assigns a data class from content and context: PII detectors, source, and domain rules. The class is stored as metadata on the datum (chunk, memory row, document) and consulted at every boundary — retrieval filters by allowed classes (chapter 14), redaction acts on sensitive classes (chapter 12), retention and residency policies key off the class. Classifying at creation is what makes the rest automatic.

In AgentVerse

  • app/data_classification/classifier.py, schema.pyclassification and the taxonomy
  • app/data_classification/redaction.pyacting on the class
  • app/memory/contracts.pyClassification on every memory row (chapter 3)
  • app/ingestion/pipeline.pyclassification at stage 4 (chapter 5)

Worked example

an ingested invoice
 classifier → confidential (contains vendor bank details, PII)
 stored as metadata on every chunk
 → retrieval excludes it for principals below confidential
 → redaction applies before external tool calls
 → retention: 7 years (regulatory) · residency: tenant region
one classification, five downstream behaviours

At production scale

Classification at creation is the difference between compliance-by-design and a permanent manual burden. Get it right at ingestion and a data-subject request or an audit is a query; skip it and both become a hunt across every store. The four-level taxonomy is deliberately simple — enough to drive behaviour, few enough that classification is reliable.

Failure modes

  • Unclassified data. Every downstream obligation becomes manual.
  • Classification only in one store. Copies losing their class.
  • Over-classification. Everything restricted, nothing usable.

Choose it when

  • Always, at creation, before the data spreads.

02Provenance#

ShippedProvenanceImpl8/10Criticality8/10

Every datum knows where it came from — which source, which document, which revision, which extraction — so a claim can be traced to evidence and a data-subject request can find every derived copy.

Mechanism

Provenance records the origin chain: a chunk cites its document and source revision (chapter 4), a memory cites its evidence (chapter 3), a claim cites the chunk that supports it (chapter 18). A source reference is the canonical pointer; a claim trace links an assertion back through the chain to its origin. This is both a grounding mechanism (verify a claim) and a compliance mechanism (find everything derived from a subject's data).

In AgentVerse

  • app/provenance/source_ref.py, claim_trace.py, ledger.pysource references and claim traces
  • app/provenance/provenance_verifier.py, export.pyverification and WORM export
  • app/ingestion/provenance_builder.pybuilding provenance at ingestion

Worked example

a data-subject request for "j.patel"
 provenance finds: 3 documents mentioning them → 41 derived chunks →
 their embeddings → 4 memory rows citing those chunks → 2 cache entries
without provenance, finding the derived copies is a manual search that
misses things — and missing a copy is a compliance failure

At production scale

Provenance is what makes both grounding and deletion tractable. For grounding, it is the chain from a claim to its evidence (chapter 18); for compliance, it is the map that a deletion cascade follows. The two uses share the same records, which is why building provenance at ingestion pays twice. WORM export preserves the chain even against tampering.

Failure modes

  • Broken chains. Derived data that cannot be traced to its origin.
  • Provenance not built at ingestion. Retrofitting it is nearly impossible.

Choose it when

  • Any data that must be verifiable or deletable.

03Retention and residency#

ShippedPolicyImpl8/10Criticality9/10

Data has a lifespan and a location: retention policies expire it when its purpose ends, residency policies keep it in the region the tenant requires — both driven by classification and compliance regime.

Mechanism

A retention policy sets an expiry per data class and regime (a HIPAA record 6 years, a raw screenshot 7 days); an archive policy moves cold data to cheaper storage before expiry; an export policy governs what may leave. Residency pins where data may live — enforced at storage and at the connector, so an EU tenant's data neither rests nor transits outside its region. Both key off the classification and the compliance bundle (chapter 13).

In AgentVerse

  • app/lifecycle/retention_policy.py, archive_policy.py, export_policy.pyretention, archival, export
  • app/governance/compliance_bundles.pyregime-driven retention (chapter 13)
  • app/scaling/memory_tasks.pythe jobs that expire and archive

Worked example

data class          retention        residency
raw screenshot      7 days           tenant region
invoice (financial) 7 years          tenant region
chat transcript     90 days          tenant region
audit entry         7 years, WORM    tenant region
an EU tenant: connectors and storage refuse to place data outside the EU

At production scale

Retention is both a compliance requirement and a cost control — data that lives forever is a growing liability and a growing bill. Residency is a hard requirement for regulated tenants and must be enforced at every layer, including the connectors that could egress data out of region and the model providers whose regions matter. Default retention to a policy, never to infinite; the row nobody set a policy on is the one an auditor finds.

Failure modes

  • Infinite retention by default. A growing liability.
  • Residency only in storage. A connector egressing out of region.
  • Retention without archival. Hot storage for cold data.

Choose it when

  • Any data about people, or any regulated tenant.

04Deletion, cascade, and legal hold#

ShippedDeletionImpl8/10Criticality10/10

Real deletion is a cascade across every store a datum was copied into — rows, embeddings, caches, traces, screenshots — provable, and suspended by legal hold where the law requires the data kept.

Mechanism

A deletion orchestrator, given a subject or a datum, uses provenance to find every derived copy and removes each: the source row, its chunks and embeddings, cache entries, memory rows, artifacts, and (per policy) trace data — producing a deletion receipt that is itself audited. Legal hold is the exception: it suspends deletion for named subjects or matters, because destroying data under hold is a worse offence than retaining it. The hold's application is audited too.

In AgentVerse

  • app/lifecycle/deletion_orchestrator.pythe cascade
  • app/lifecycle/legal_hold_policy.py, app/governance/legal_holds.pylegal hold
  • app/api/dpdp.pydata-subject request surface
  • app/memory/postgres_repository.pymemory deletion (chapter 3)

Worked example

deletion request: subject "j.patel@example"
 legal hold check → none active for this subject
 provenance → 3 docs, 41 chunks, 41 embeddings, 4 memories, 2 cache entries,
   2 screenshots
 orchestrator deletes all → deletion receipt (counts, timestamps) → audited
if a hold were active: deletion SUSPENDED, the requester informed, the hold
 recorded

At production scale

Deletion is much harder than it looks because a datum has been copied into six or seven stores, and missing one is a compliance failure that surfaces at the worst time. Design the cascade on day one — retrofitting a deletion path across every store is a quarter of work, and provenance is what makes it possible at all. Legal hold is the correct exception mechanism: it must be able to override deletion, and its own application must be audited. Full memory-deletion treatment in chapter 3.

Failure modes

  • Orphaned embeddings. The row deleted, the vector still retrievable.
  • Cached content. A deleted datum served from cache.
  • Deletion during hold. Destroying evidence under legal hold.
  • No proof. Deletion you cannot demonstrate.

Choose it when

  • Always. Any system holding data about people needs this before the first row.

What is missing here

  • Automated classification accuracy monitoring. Classification drives everything, but its accuracy is not continuously measured against human-corrected samples.
  • Deletion verification. The cascade produces a receipt; an independent verification pass that confirms nothing survived is thin.
  • Cross-region data movement audit. Residency is enforced; a continuous audit that no datum crossed a region boundary is future work.
  • Consent lineage. Provenance tracks data origin; linking each datum to the consent that permits its processing is not first-class.
