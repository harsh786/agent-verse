Orientation

Chunking is the highest-leverage, least-glamorous decision in a knowledge system. The chunk is the unit of retrieval, so the boundaries you cut determine what can ever be found: a chunk that splits a table from its header can never answer a question about that table, and no reranker, bigger model or cleverer pattern will fix it.

Two forces pull in opposite directions. Precision wants small chunks — a 200-token chunk about refunds matches a refund query cleanly. Sufficiency wants large chunks — the model needs enough context to answer. Every strategy below is a different resolution of that tension, and the good ones (parent-child, late chunking) refuse the trade entirely by decoupling what you index from what you return.

The strategies

01Fixed-size chunking#

ShippedBaselineImpl9/10Criticality6/10

Cut every n characters. Fast, predictable, structure-blind — and the right answer only as a fallback when you know nothing about the content.

Mechanism

Split the text at fixed character intervals with a fixed overlap. No parsing, no model, no assumptions. Its single virtue is that it always terminates with predictable chunk counts, which makes it a safe default for unknown content and a useful control in evaluations.

Its defect is that character counts have no relationship to meaning or to tokens: a cut lands mid-sentence, mid-word, mid-number.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunking_strategy_selector.py_fixed_chunk() — the fallback path
  • app/rag/chunker.pybasic chunking

Worked example

size 500, overlap 50
 …"the refund window is 30 da"   | "ys from delivery. Damaged"…
                                  ↑ cut mid-word, mid-fact
retrieval for "refund window" matches chunk 1, which does not contain
the answer's second half

At production scale

Keep it as the fallback and as the eval baseline, never as the default for content you understand. When a new format arrives and the classifier cannot place it, fixed chunking is what stops the pipeline failing — that is its job.

Failure modes

  • Mid-sentence cuts. Both chunks are weaker than either would have been.
  • Split entities. An account number or date cut in half.
  • Character/token mismatch. 500 characters is 90 tokens of English and 400 tokens of code or CJK text.

Choose it when

  • Fallback for unknown content; baseline for evaluation.
  • Never as the production default for a known format.

02Token-aware chunking#

ShippedBaselineImpl8/10Criticality8/10

Cut on token counts using the actual tokeniser, and prefer natural separators — the sane default when structure is unavailable.

Mechanism

Count with the tokeniser the embedding model uses, and walk a separator hierarchy: paragraph break, then sentence end, then clause, then word, then character. Take the largest separator that keeps the chunk within budget. This yields chunks that fit the model exactly and rarely cut mid-thought.

Token accuracy matters because embedding models truncate silently. A "1,000 character" chunk of dense technical text can exceed an 8,192-token limit in a way nobody notices until retrieval quality drops for long documents only.

In AgentVerse

  • app/agent/tokenizer.pytokenisation used for budgeting
  • app/ingestion/chunkers/base.pychunker interface and separator handling
  • app/rag/chunker.pytoken-aware splitting

Worked example

target 512 tokens, overlap 64
paragraph boundary at 480 tokens → cut there (not at 512)
next chunk starts 64 tokens back, at a sentence boundary
result: chunks of 380–512 tokens, none cut mid-sentence

At production scale

Use the real tokeniser, not a characters-divided-by-four estimate. The estimate is wrong by 3–5× on code, JSON and non-Latin scripts, and every downstream budget (context, cost, truncation) inherits the error.

Failure modes

  • Estimated tokens. Silent truncation at the embedder.
  • Wrong tokeniser. Counting with one model's tokeniser and embedding with another.
  • Separator starvation. Text with no paragraph or sentence breaks falls through to character splitting.

Choose it when

  • Structure is unknown or unreliable — the honest default.

03Semantic chunking#

PartialContent-awareImpl7/10Criticality6/10

Cut where the topic changes: embed sentences, measure similarity between neighbours, and split at the dips.

Mechanism

Embed each sentence, compute similarity between consecutive sentences, and place a boundary where similarity drops below a threshold (or below a percentile of the document's distribution). Enforce minimum and maximum sizes so a boundary-free passage still gets split and a rapid topic oscillation does not produce fifty tiny chunks.

Cost is one embedding per sentence at index time — significant on a large corpus, and the main reason this is not the universal default.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/semantic.pySemanticChunker
  • app/ingestion/chunking_strategy_selector.pyselected for unstructured prose

Worked example

sentence-to-sentence similarity
 s1→s2  0.81   s2→s3  0.78   s3→s4  0.34  ← topic shift: boundary
 s4→s5  0.85   s5→s6  0.80   s6→s7  0.29  ← boundary
chunks [s1-s3] refund mechanics · [s4-s6] warranty terms · [s7-…] returns
a token-based chunker would have cut in the middle of the warranty section

At production scale

Worth it for unstructured prose that matters — transcripts of meetings, long-form reports, articles. Not worth it for content that already has headings (use them) or for corpora where paragraph-level chunking already evaluates well. Measure on your own data: the gain over token-aware chunking is real but often modest, and the index-time cost is not.

Failure modes

  • Threshold sensitivity. Slightly wrong and you get either one giant chunk or hundreds of fragments — always enforce min/max.
  • Embedding cost. Per-sentence embedding on a million documents.
  • Bad sentence splitting. Garbage in, garbage boundaries out.

Choose it when

  • Prose with no structural markers, and quality justifies the index cost.
  • Skip when headings exist.

04Markdown and heading chunking#

ShippedContent-awareImpl9/10Criticality8/10

Use the outline the author already wrote. Split at headings, carry the heading path into metadata, never split a code fence or a table.

Mechanism

Parse the heading hierarchy, then cut at a chosen level (usually H2), recursively splitting any section that exceeds the size budget at the next level down and finally by tokens. The heading path travels with the chunk — both as retrieval-time context and as a filter field.

Atomic elements are protected: code fences, tables and list blocks are never split, even if that makes a chunk oversized.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/heading.pyHeadingChunker
  • app/ingestion/parsers/markdown_parser.pythe parsed hierarchy
  • app/ingestion/parsers/docx_parser.pysame treatment for Word heading styles

Worked example

# Payments runbook
## Payout failures                     → chunk 1  (path: [Runbook, Payout failures])
### Diagnosis            (2,400 tok)   → too big, split at H3
  → chunk 2  (path: [… , Diagnosis])       880 tok
  → chunk 3  (path: [… , Diagnosis])       760 tok  (token split, sentence bdry)
  ```bash …```                            kept whole inside chunk 2
## Escalation                          → chunk 4

At production scale

Heading chunking is the best quality-per-effort strategy for internal documentation, which is usually your highest-signal corpus. The heading path in metadata pays twice: it gives the model context and gives users a navigable citation.

Failure modes

  • Documents with one heading. Falls back to token splitting — that is correct, but size the fallback deliberately.
  • Split code fences. Always a bug.
  • Lost path. A chunk saying "run the second command" with no context.

Choose it when

  • Any authored document with real headings — start here.

05Code-aware (AST) chunking#

ShippedContent-awareImpl8/10Criticality8/10

One chunk per function, method or class, with the enclosing signature and imports attached — because a syntactic unit is the only meaningful unit of code.

Mechanism

Parse to a syntax tree and emit chunks at declaration boundaries. Attach the context a reader needs: module imports, the enclosing class signature for a method, decorators, and the docstring. Oversized functions split at statement boundaries rather than mid-expression. Metadata carries language, path, symbol name and revision.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/ast_chunker.pyASTChunker
  • app/ingestion/chunking_strategy_selector.pyselected for code content types
  • app/embedding/router.pycode chunks routed to a code embedding model

Worked example

chunk
  # from app.security import verify_hmac        ← imports carried
  class WebhookHandler:                          ← enclosing signature
      def handle(self, event: Event) -> None:
          """Dispatch a verified webhook."""
          …
  metadata {lang:python, symbol:WebhookHandler.handle, path:…, rev:a91f2}

without imports and class context, the chunk is unrunnable knowledge:
you cannot tell what verify_hmac is or what self refers to

At production scale

Exclude generated and vendored code aggressively — dependencies can outnumber first-party code ten to one and will dominate retrieval. Re-chunk on merge so symbol metadata matches the revision an agent is reasoning about.

Failure modes

  • Unparseable files. Syntax errors, unsupported languages — fall back to token chunking rather than dropping the file.
  • Missing context. A method without its class.
  • Giant functions. A 2,000-line function still needs internal splitting.

Choose it when

  • Any code corpus. Never use a prose chunker on code.

06PDF layout chunking#

PartialContent-awareImpl7/10Criticality8/10

Reconstruct reading order from geometry before splitting — columns, blocks, headers and footers are the difference between usable text and noise.

Mechanism

Group characters into lines, lines into blocks, blocks into columns using coordinates; determine reading order; identify and drop repeated headers and footers; keep figure captions with their figures and table cells with their headers. Then chunk within the reconstructed flow, carrying the page number so citations can point at a page.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/pdf_layout.pylayout-aware chunking
  • app/ingestion/parsers/pdf_parser.pygeometry and text extraction
  • app/ingestion/chunkers/table.pytables handled separately

Worked example

two-column page
naive order  L1 R1 L2 R2 L3 R3 …   ← lines interleaved across columns
layout order L1 L2 L3 … then R1 R2 R3 …
dropped      "ACME Confidential — page 14 of 84" (repeated on every page)
kept         "Figure 3: settlement flow" with its figure reference

At production scale

PDF layout handling has the highest ratio of quality gained to engineering spent of anything in ingestion, because interleaved-column text is both common and completely unusable. Measure it directly: sample extracted text and read it. If a human cannot follow it, no model will.

Failure modes

  • Column interleaving. The defining PDF failure.
  • Header pollution. The same company name in every chunk.
  • Table flattening. Numbers separated from their labels.
  • Lost page numbers. Citations that cannot be followed.

Choose it when

  • Any PDF corpus with multi-column or complex layout — that is, most reports.

07Table chunking#

PartialContent-awareImpl7/10Criticality8/10

Every chunk of a table must carry the header row, or the numbers in it are meaningless — and a large table should be summarised rather than split into a hundred fragments.

Mechanism

Small tables become one chunk, serialised as markdown or key-value rows. Large tables split into row groups with the header repeated in each group, plus a leading summary chunk describing the table's columns, ranges and purpose. Very large tables should not be chunked at all: index the schema and summary, and answer questions with code execution against the data.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/table.pyTableChunker
  • app/ingestion/parsers/excel_parser.py, csv_parser.pytable extraction
  • app/tools/code_interpreter.pythe alternative for large tables

Worked example

table: quarterly revenue by region, 4 columns × 60 rows

split without headers
  chunk 2: "APAC | 4,120 | 3,890 | 5,010"    ← 4,120 of what? which quarter?
split with headers repeated
  chunk 2: "Region | Q1 | Q2 | Q3
            APAC   | 4,120 | 3,890 | 5,010"  ← answerable
plus summary chunk: "Quarterly revenue by region, FY24, INR lakhs,
                     12 regions, Q1–Q4 columns"

At production scale

Financial and operational tables are where confidently wrong answers come from, and the cause is almost always a header separated from its values. Treat header preservation as an invariant and test it. For anything above a few hundred rows, the schema-plus-code-execution route is both cheaper and more accurate.

Failure modes

  • Headers dropped. Numbers without labels.
  • Merged cells. Misaligned columns.
  • Row-per-chunk. Thousands of near-identical vectors.
  • Units lost. "4,120" without knowing it is lakhs.

Choose it when

  • Documents contain tables that matter — reports, statements, specifications.
  • Do not chunk large datasets; query them.

08JSON and CSV chunking#

ShippedContent-awareImpl8/10Criticality6/10

Either one chunk per meaningful record, or a flattened path: value representation — the shape of the data decides which.

Mechanism

For record-shaped data (an array of objects), one chunk per record, serialised as labelled fields, is usually right — each record is a retrievable entity. For configuration-shaped data (deep nested objects), flattening to a.b.c: value lines preserves the structure in a form embeddings can use. For very large arrays, do neither: index the schema and a sample.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/parsers/json_parser.pyflattening and record detection
  • app/ingestion/parsers/csv_parser.py, yaml_parser.pytabular and config formats
  • app/ingestion/parsers/parquet_parser.py, avro_parser.pycolumnar formats

Worked example

record-shaped  [{id, customer, amount, status}, …]
  chunk per record: "id: INV-8891 · customer: Northwind ·
                     amount: 41,200 INR · status: disputed"

config-shaped  {"services":{"payments":{"retries":3,"timeout":30}}}
  flattened:  services.payments.retries: 3
              services.payments.timeout: 30
query "what is the payments timeout?" → matches the flattened path

At production scale

Configuration corpora (Kubernetes manifests, service configs, feature flags) are an underrated knowledge source and flatten beautifully. Data exports are the opposite: they belong in a queryable store, not an embedding index.

Failure modes

  • Embedding raw JSON. Braces and quotes dominate the tokens.
  • Flattening huge arrays. Path explosion.
  • Type loss. Everything becomes a string; no comparisons possible.

Choose it when

  • Config and record data are part of the corpus; not for bulk data.

09Timestamp chunking#

ShippedContent-awareImpl8/10Criticality6/10

For transcripts, cut on time windows with overlap and keep the time range in metadata — so a citation can point at a moment, not a document.

Mechanism

Split the transcript into windows (60–120 seconds is a good default) with 10–20% overlap so a sentence spanning a boundary is not lost. Keep start and end times, speaker labels where available, and the recording identifier. Prefer speaker turns as boundaries when diarisation is reliable — a turn is a better semantic unit than a clock interval.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/timestamp.pyTimestampChunker
  • app/ingestion/parsers/audio_parser.pytranscript with timings
  • app/voice/stt_engine.pythe transcription source

Worked example

48-minute call → 90s windows, 15s overlap → 34 chunks
chunk 15  t 21:00–22:30  speaker=customer
  "…we'd need the renewal moved to April because our budget cycle…"
citation  "sales-call-2026-09-02, 21:00" → playable link
query "when does their budget cycle start?" → this chunk, this moment

At production scale

The timestamp is the feature. A citation into a two-hour recording that says "somewhere in this file" is worthless; one that opens at 21:00 is immediately verifiable, and verifiability is what makes people trust agent output from recordings.

Failure modes

  • No overlap. Sentences cut at window boundaries.
  • Fixed windows through speaker changes. Two speakers in one chunk, attribution lost.
  • Missing timings. The chunk is just text again.

Choose it when

  • Any audio or video transcript.

10Scene chunking#

PartialContent-awareImpl6/10Criticality4/10

For video, cut on scene changes rather than the clock, so each chunk holds one coherent visual context plus the words spoken over it.

Mechanism

Detect scene boundaries from frame differences, then build a chunk per scene containing the transcript for that interval, a keyframe description, and any on-screen text from OCR. The result is a chunk where audio and visual content agree, which is what makes video retrieval answer questions rather than return timestamps.

Image-region chunking — splitting a single image into semantically distinct regions and indexing each — is the natural extension and is not implemented here; the OCR extractors work at the whole-document level (chapter 21).

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/scene.pySceneChunker
  • app/ingestion/parsers/video_parser.pyscene detection and frame extraction

Worked example

12-minute demo → 9 scenes
scene 4  3:40–5:10
  speech  "and here you configure the retry policy"
  frame   OCR "Retry attempts: 3 · Backoff: exponential"
  chunk   both, with t_start=3:40
"what's the default retry count?" → answered from the frame text

At production scale

One keyframe per scene, not one per second. The frame budget is the whole cost story for video, and scenes are the natural sampling unit — a slide that stays on screen for four minutes needs one caption, not two hundred.

Failure modes

  • Over-detection. A camera pan producing forty "scenes".
  • Under-detection. A 20-minute screencast as one scene.
  • Audio/visual drift. Transcript and frames misaligned.

Choose it when

  • Video where the screen carries information.
  • For talking-head video, timestamp chunking on the transcript is enough.

11Agentic chunking#

PartialModel-drivenImpl6/10Criticality5/10

Let a model decide the boundaries — reading the document and cutting where a human would. Best quality, highest cost, hardest to make deterministic.

Mechanism

A model reads the document (or a window of it) and returns proposed boundaries with reasons, optionally plus a title and summary per chunk. Because the same document should chunk identically every time, the output must be cached and versioned by document hash and prompt version — otherwise re-ingestion reshuffles every chunk id and invalidates every citation.

In AgentVerse

  • app/rag/agentic/patterns/agentic_chunking.pythe strategy (also exposed as RAGStrategy.AGENTIC_CHUNKING)
  • app/rag/contracts.pypart of the public strategy vocabulary

Rated 6: implemented as a strategy, but the determinism and caching discipline needed to run it over a large corpus is not fully in place.

Worked example

a court judgment with no headings
model boundaries
  1 procedural history      "distinct narrative, ends at 'the appellant then'"
  2 facts as found
  3 legal reasoning
  4 order and costs
a token chunker cuts arbitrarily through the reasoning section, which is
the part every query is about

At production scale

Reserve it for small, high-value, structurally-hostile corpora: judgments, contracts, medical notes. On a million documents it is prohibitively expensive, and its quality edge over heading or semantic chunking on well-structured content is small. Cache by document hash so you pay once.

Failure modes

  • Non-determinism. New boundaries on every ingest; citations rot. Cache and version.
  • Cost at scale. One call per document minimum, often more.
  • Boundary drift with prompt changes. Version the prompt as part of the cache key.

Choose it when

  • Small corpus, high value, no usable structure.
  • Never as a default.

12Late chunking#

PartialModel-drivenImpl7/10Criticality6/10

Embed the whole document first, then pool the token embeddings into chunks — so every chunk's vector already knows the document's context, without a per-chunk model call.

Mechanism

Conventional chunking embeds each chunk in isolation, so a chunk saying "the limit is 30 days" has no idea it is about refunds. Late chunking runs the long-context embedding model over the full document, keeps the token-level output, then averages token vectors within each chunk boundary. Each chunk vector is contextualised by everything around it — the benefit of contextual enrichment without the per-chunk generation cost.

The constraint is model support: you need an embedding model that exposes token-level output and handles long inputs.

In AgentVerse

  • app/rag/late_chunker.pythe implementation
  • app/rag/contextual_enricher.pythe generation-based alternative

Worked example

chunk text  "The limit is 30 days from delivery."
isolated embedding    → close to any document about limits or delivery
late-chunked embedding → close to refund questions, because the document
                          context (a refund policy) is baked into the vector
no extra model calls: one long-context pass over the document

At production scale

The best cost/quality point available for context-poor chunks, when your embedding model supports it. It also composes with everything else: you can late-chunk heading-based boundaries, which gives you authored structure and contextual vectors together.

Failure modes

  • Model does not support it. Needs token-level embeddings; not all providers expose them.
  • Document exceeds the context window. Must be windowed, which partially defeats it.
  • Pooling choice. Naive mean pooling over a long span dilutes the signal.

Choose it when

  • Chunks are small and context-dependent, and the model supports token output.

13Chunk overlap#

ShippedParameterImpl9/10Criticality7/10

Repeat the last n tokens of each chunk at the start of the next, so a fact spanning a boundary appears whole in at least one chunk. Typically 10–20%.

Mechanism

Overlap is insurance against boundary placement. Too little and cross-boundary facts are lost; too much and you inflate index size, duplicate retrieval hits, and waste context showing the model the same sentences twice. The right amount depends on how well your boundaries respect meaning: heading chunking needs little overlap, fixed chunking needs a lot.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunkers/base.pyoverlap handling
  • app/ingestion/pipeline.pystage 11 dedup limits the duplicate-retrieval side effect

Worked example

overlap 0
  c1 "…refunds are accepted within"
  c2 "30 days of delivery…"          neither chunk answers the question
overlap 15%
  c1 "…refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery."   ← complete
  c2 "…within 30 days of delivery. Damaged goods…"
cost: index size +15%, and both chunks may be retrieved (dedup handles it)

At production scale

Overlap is a straight multiplier on index size and embedding cost, so it deserves an explicit choice rather than a copied default. With structure-respecting boundaries, 10% is plenty. With fixed-size chunking, 20% is a patch over a worse decision.

Failure modes

  • Zero overlap on naive boundaries. Facts lost at every cut.
  • Excessive overlap. 50% overlap doubles the index for marginal gain.
  • Duplicate context. Two overlapping chunks in one prompt; deduplicate at assembly.

Choose it when

  • Always some, sized to how much you trust your boundaries.

14Chunk metadata#

ShippedParameterImpl8/10Criticality9/10

A chunk without metadata is a floating string. Position, provenance, structure and governance fields are what make it filterable, citable and deletable.

Mechanism

Every chunk inherits document metadata and adds its own: order index, heading path or page or symbol, token count, parent reference, and the chunking strategy and version that produced it. That last field is what lets you find and re-chunk everything produced by a strategy you have since improved.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/pipeline.pymetadata carried through chunk and enrich
  • app/rag/indexing.pyRAGIndexRecord — what is persisted
  • app/rag/contextual_enricher.pyderived metadata

Worked example

{ chunk_id: "acme:conf:POL-118#refunds#2",
  doc_id: "acme:conf:POL-118", order: 2, parent: "…#refunds",
  heading_path: ["Policies","Refunds"], page: null,
  tokens: 412, strategy: "heading@v3", lang: "en",
  classification: "internal", jurisdiction: "DE",
  source_ref: "confluence://space/POL/page/118?version=7" }

"re-chunk everything produced by heading@v2" is now a single query

At production scale

Record the strategy and its version on every chunk. Without it, improving a chunker means re-ingesting entire collections blindly; with it, you can migrate incrementally and compare old against new on the same corpus.

Failure modes

  • Metadata lost at chunk time. Document fields not copied down.
  • No strategy version. No migration path.
  • No parent reference. Parent-child retrieval becomes impossible after the fact.

Choose it when

  • Always. It costs bytes and saves quarters.

15The strategy selector#

ShippedDecision frameworkImpl8/10Criticality8/10

One component maps content type plus collection configuration to a chunker, so the pipeline never grows a chain of format conditionals.

Mechanism

The selector takes the classified content type and the collection's configured preference and returns a chunker instance, falling back through progressively more general strategies to fixed chunking. It exposes both select() (get the chunker) and select_and_chunk() (do it), which keeps the pipeline stage a one-liner and makes the mapping independently testable.

In AgentVerse

  • app/ingestion/chunking_strategy_selector.pyChunkingStrategySelector.select() / select_and_chunk(), dispatching to SemanticChunker, ASTChunker, HeadingChunker, TableChunker, TimestampChunker, SceneChunker, PDF layout, or _fixed_chunk()
  • app/ingestion/pipeline.pystage 8 calls it
  • app/ingestion/orchestrator.pythe orchestrated path

Worked example

content type            → chunker
text/markdown           → HeadingChunker
text/x-python           → ASTChunker
application/pdf (text)  → pdf_layout
application/pdf (scan)  → OCR path, then heading/semantic on the result
text/csv                → TableChunker
audio/*                 → TimestampChunker
video/*                 → SceneChunker
text/plain              → SemanticChunker (or token-aware if configured)
unknown                 → _fixed_chunk()
collection override always wins over the content-type default

At production scale

Make the mapping data, not code, so a tenant with unusual content can be reconfigured without a deploy. And make the fallback loud: a rising share of documents landing on fixed chunking means your classifier or your parser coverage has a gap.

Failure modes

  • Silent fallback. Everything quietly fixed-chunked.
  • Format conditionals in the pipeline. The thing the selector exists to prevent.
  • Mixed strategies in one collection. Inconsistent chunk sizes make ranking harder to reason about.

Choose it when

  • More than one content type — immediately.

16Measuring chunking quality#

PartialMeasurementImpl6/10Criticality8/10

Chunking is the one parameter you should tune empirically, because the right answer is corpus-specific and the wrong answer is invisible.

Mechanism

Build a small labelled set — questions with the passage that answers each — then measure, per strategy and per size: answer containment (does a retrieved chunk actually contain the answer), recall@k, and context efficiency (answer tokens as a share of retrieved tokens). Sweep size and overlap, and pick the point where containment plateaus.

The cheap proxy when you have no labels: sample chunks and read them. If a chunk is not self-explanatory to a human, it will not be to a model.

In AgentVerse

  • app/rag/evaluation.pyretrieval evaluation
  • app/evals/rag_score.pyretrieval scoring
  • app/evals/dataset_builder.pybuilding the labelled set
  • app/api/golden_datasets.pymanaging golden data

Rated 6: the evaluation components exist, but there is no built-in chunking sweep that reports containment by strategy and size for a collection.

Worked example

corpus: 400 policy documents · 120 labelled questions

strategy        size  overlap  containment  recall@5  ctx efficiency
fixed            500     50       0.61        0.72       0.09
token-aware      512     64       0.74        0.81       0.11
heading         auto     10%      0.88        0.93       0.14
heading+parent  300/1200 10%      0.91        0.95       0.08
semantic        auto     10%      0.86        0.91       0.13

decision: heading (best quality per token); parent-child for the
regulated subset where completeness beats efficiency

At production scale

Run this once per corpus type and you will save more quality than a model upgrade would buy. Re-run when the corpus changes character — a collection that started as wiki pages and became PDF reports needs a different answer.

Keep the labelled set small and real: 100–200 questions from actual user queries beats a thousand synthetic ones.

Failure modes

  • Copying someone else's parameters. 512/64 is a starting point, not a conclusion.
  • Optimising recall only. High recall with terrible context efficiency blows your budget.
  • Never re-measuring. Chunking tuned for the corpus you had two years ago.

Choose it when

  • Before you ingest a million documents with parameters nobody validated.

What is missing here

  • Re-chunking without re-fetching. Parsed text is not retained, so changing a strategy means going back to the source. Storing parsed output would make chunking migrations cheap.
  • Image-region chunking. Images are handled whole; splitting a diagram into labelled regions and indexing each is not implemented.
  • A built-in chunking sweep. The evaluation parts exist; the "try five strategies on this collection and show me containment" workflow does not.
  • Deterministic agentic chunking. Needs hash-and-prompt-versioned caching before it is safe on a large corpus.
  • Cross-document chunk linking. No representation for "this chunk supersedes that one", which is what a policy corpus with versions actually needs.
